# Resume review and project interview

## Begin with the evidence

Read the supplied resume completely. Identify target role and language if supplied, the requested editing scope, and the strongest relevant experiences. Inspect linked or local project material only within authorized access. Do not assume that a publicly visible project belongs to the applicant or that a repository feature was their personal work.

Use a private working ledger when substantive edits need it:

| Claim | Basis | Uncertainty | Draft treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owned a component | Applicant's account | Team scope unclear | Preserve ownership wording; ask scope question |
| Reduced latency | User-provided benchmark | Baseline or percentile missing | Retain stated context; request definition before strengthening |
| Implemented retry logic | Code and tests | Deployment unknown | Describe implementation; avoid production-impact claim |

A user-provided figure is a valid attributed source for drafting. Do not replace its definition with a different statistic found in research. If evidence conflicts, explain the exact conflict and ask a targeted question; retain the approved wording pending resolution when appropriate. Mark new unconfirmed proposals in review notes, not as achievements in the final resume.

## Ask questions that change a bullet

Ask a small batch focused on the largest evidence gaps. Avoid a generic questionnaire when the materials already answer it. Useful questions include:

- What specific user or operational problem made this work necessary, and what failed before your change?
- Which part did you personally design, implement, test, or operate? What belonged to teammates?
- What was the difficult technical decision? Which alternative did you reject, and why?
- What constraints mattered: scale, latency, memory, consistency, compatibility, cost, deadline, or failure recovery?
- How was the result measured: baseline, data size, workload, time window, environment, and statistic?
- Was it a prototype, controlled test, shipped feature, or production rollout? What evidence supports that stage?
- What failed during development, and how did you identify and fix it?

Follow up on mechanisms instead of soliciting impressive numbers. If the applicant has no reliable metric, a precise description of scope, failure mode, design decision, and verified behavior can be stronger than a fabricated percentage. Do not force disclosure of employer-confidential data; accept qualitative or authorized aggregate descriptions.

## Improve the draft

Aim for distinct evidence of relevant skills across bullets. A useful shape is **problem → personal action and technical decision → supported result**, with context retained where it changes the claim. Vary the shape naturally rather than forcing every line into a formula.

- Prefer concrete mechanisms over broad claims such as “optimized architecture.”
- Keep the meaning of numbers intact: rows stored are not ingest throughput; a target is not a measured result; test traffic is not production traffic.
- Do not combine best values from separate experiments into an imaginary final benchmark.
- Distinguish team outcomes from individual contribution, and implementation from deployment.
- Remove repeated summaries and technology lists before removing meaningful constraints or caveats.
- Preserve terminology needed for the target role, but do not invent experience to match a job description.

Review edits against the original for names, roles, dates, metrics, units, scope, and action strength. Provide a concise explanation of substantive changes and unresolved questions. When answers are still pending, deliver the supported draft and identify exactly which additions await those answers. Formatting work can proceed independently.

## Language variants

Maintain separate editable sources for separate languages unless the project intentionally uses another model. Translate responsibility and certainty accurately: “contributed” must not become “led,” and a controlled result must not become a production claim. Review dates, units, organization names, and metric definitions across languages. Do not claim both languages are current merely because both compile.
